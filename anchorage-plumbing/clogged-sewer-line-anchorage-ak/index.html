    Frequently Asked Questions About Clogged Sewer Line in Anchorage

    Most main line clearings take one to two hours. Severe root intrusion or lines without a cleanout can take longer. Camera inspection adds about 30 to 60 minutes.

    While minor clogs in individual drains can sometimes be resolved with DIY methods, a main sewer line clog is best handled by professionals. Attempting to clear it yourself without the right tools can push the blockage further or damage your pipes, especially with older plumbing systems.

    Multiple drains backing up at once, gurgling toilets when the washer drains and sewage odors in the house are classic signs. Water rising in the shower when you flush a toilet is another strong clue. Wet spots or sewage smell near the cleanout outdoors can also signal a clog.

    Common signs include multiple drains backing up at once, sewage odors coming from drains, gurgling sounds from toilets or drains, and water backing up in unusual places like the shower when the toilet is flushed. These indicate a blockage further down your main sewer line, not just a localized drain issue.

    Homeowners are typically responsible for the lateral from the house to the property line or city tap. If the clog is in the city main, the municipality handles it. Our camera and locator help determine where the blockage is.
