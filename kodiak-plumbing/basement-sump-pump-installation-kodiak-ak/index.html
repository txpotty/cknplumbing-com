    New Sump Pit Excavation in Kodiak

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by finding the low point where water collects. We saw cut the slab, excavate a pit roughly 18 to 24 inches wide and 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a perforated or solid liner depending on whether we are collecting groundwater or surface seepage. Gravel goes around the liner to help water flow in while keeping fine soil out. We then patch the slab flush with new concrete so the floor remains clean and level.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Kodiak

    Power outages during heavy storms can render a primary sump pump useless, leading to basement flooding. A battery backup sump pump provides crucial protection when the power goes out. We install reliable backup systems that activate automatically, keeping water moving even without electricity.     Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Kodiak

    A great pump is useless if the water it moves runs right back toward the foundation. We route rigid PVC discharge pipe out of the basement and extend it at least 10 feet from the house or into a pop-up emitter or dry well. A spring or swing check valve keeps the column of water from dropping back into the pit after every cycle, which saves wear on the motor. Homes here commonly use heat tape, insulated chases, and supply lines buried deep below the frost line to keep water moving all winter. Where lines can freeze, we add a freeze-resistant outlet so ice cannot block the discharge.
